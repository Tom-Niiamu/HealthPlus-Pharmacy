The Best Times of Year to Get a Health Checkup in Ghana

March 2026  ·  3 min read

Prevention March 2026 3 min read
Dr. Kwame MensahGeneral Practitioner · HealthPlus Pharmacy

Ghana's climate, seasonal disease patterns, and public health calendar create predictable periods when certain health risks spike. Planning your screenings and checkups around these patterns is a practical, low-effort way to stay ahead of illness rather than chasing it.

The Rainy Season (April–July, September–November): Malaria and Respiratory Infections

Malaria transmission in Ghana peaks during and immediately after the rainy season, when standing water creates ideal mosquito breeding conditions. This is the most important time of year to ensure your malaria prevention strategy is current — whether that is sleeping under treated nets, clearing standing water around your home, or taking prophylaxis if you are in a high-risk group.

Rainy season checklist

✓ Confirm your mosquito net is intact and treated
✓ Remove any standing water near your home
✓ If you develop fever, chills or severe headache — test for malaria within 24 hours, not "after a couple of days"

45%

of outpatient consultations in Ghana during the rainy season are malaria-related.

24h

is the window for malaria testing from fever onset — earlier treatment significantly improves outcomes.

The Harmattan Season (November–March): Skin, Eyes and Respiratory Health

The Harmattan winds bring dry, dusty air from the Sahara across Ghana. This period sees a spike in dry skin, chapped lips, eye irritation, nosebleeds and — most significantly — respiratory conditions. Asthma and COPD patients consistently report worse symptoms during the Harmattan, and meningitis risk increases in the northern regions.

This is the right time to have your lung function checked if you have not done so recently, and to review your asthma medication with your doctor.

January–February: The Ideal Annual Checkup Window

The post-festive period — after the high-calorie, high-alcohol December celebrations — is one of the best times for a comprehensive annual health check. Blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol and liver function often show measurable changes after the festive period, and catching these early sets you up for the rest of the year.

1

Full blood count

Screens for anaemia, infection and blood disorders. Particularly important for women of reproductive age.

2

Blood glucose (fasting)

Catches pre-diabetes and diabetes before symptoms develop. Recommended annually over age 35.

3

Blood pressure measurement

The single most important number to know. Hypertension causes no symptoms — you cannot feel it until it damages something.

4

Cholesterol panel

Particularly important if you have a family history of heart disease or have not been checked in the last 2 years.

Year-Round: Don't Wait for Symptoms

Preventive care is more cost-effective than treating disease that has been left to progress.
