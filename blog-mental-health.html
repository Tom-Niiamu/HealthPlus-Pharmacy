Breaking the Silence: Seeking Mental Health Help in Ghana

May 2026  ·  6 min read

Mental Health May 2026 6 min read
Dr. Efua DarkoClinical Psychologist · HealthPlus Pharmacy

For many Ghanaians, admitting to mental health struggles feels like a confession of weakness, spiritual failure, or family shame. These beliefs are deeply rooted — but they are also costing lives. Depression is a leading cause of disability worldwide, and in Ghana, the treatment gap between those who need care and those who receive it is estimated at over 90%.

Something is beginning to shift. A younger generation is speaking more openly. Social media has created spaces where Ghanaians share their experiences with anxiety and depression without always hiding behind anonymity. Telemedicine has made confidential, affordable therapy accessible for the first time to people who would never walk through the door of a psychiatric unit.

What Mental Health Actually Means

Mental health is not the absence of sadness or stress. It is the ability to function — to work, to maintain relationships, to handle the ordinary difficulties of life — without being overwhelmed by them. When that ability is consistently impaired, something medical is happening, not something spiritual or moral.

1 in 5

Ghanaian adults will experience a diagnosable mental health condition at some point in their lives.

90%

treatment gap — the proportion of people with mental health conditions in Ghana who receive no professional support.

Common Conditions — and What They Feel Like

Depression

Not sadness — though sadness is part of it. Clinical depression is a persistent loss of interest or pleasure in almost everything, combined with fatigue, difficulty concentrating, sleep changes, and sometimes thoughts of self-harm or death. It is a medical illness with effective treatments. Untreated, it tends to worsen over time.

Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety becomes a disorder when the fear and worry is disproportionate to the actual situation and consistently interferes with daily life. It manifests as racing heart, difficulty breathing, avoidance of situations, and persistent catastrophic thoughts. It is not "overthinking" and it is not solved by "just relaxing."

Post-Traumatic Stress (PTSD)

PTSD is not only a condition of soldiers. Road accidents, sexual violence, childhood abuse, sudden bereavement — any traumatic event can leave a psychological imprint that, without treatment, becomes a disorder. Flashbacks, nightmares, emotional numbness, and hypervigilance are hallmarks.

How to recognise when you or someone you know needs support

Two or more weeks of persistent low mood, inability to function at work or in relationships, changes in sleep and appetite, withdrawal from people you previously enjoyed spending time with, or any thoughts of self-harm — these all warrant a conversation with a professional.

How to Take the First Step

1

Acknowledge it to yourself

You do not need to label yourself or have a diagnosis. Simply accepting that you have been struggling — and that you deserve support — is the most important step.

2

Start with a trusted person

A friend, a family member, or a pastor you trust. You do not need to share everything — just that you have not been well and are thinking of speaking to someone.

3

Reach out to a licensed professional

A doctor, psychologist or counsellor can help you understand what you're experiencing and recommend next steps, confidentially and without judgment.

4

Be honest about what you're experiencing

The more honestly you describe what you are feeling, the better the support you will receive. If medication is prescribed, HealthPlus Pharmacy can have it verified and delivered to you.

If you or someone you know is in crisis

Please reach out to a mental health helpline or emergency services in your area right away, or go to your nearest hospital. You are not alone, and help is available right now.
