How to Safely Store Medication at Home in Ghana's Climate

March 2026  ·  4 min read

Pharmacy March 2026 4 min read
Abena GyasiRegistered Pharmacist · HealthPlus Pharmacy

Ghana's average temperature hovers between 28–34°C, with humidity often exceeding 70%. These conditions are extremely unfriendly to many medications. The "cool, dry place" instruction printed on most drug packaging was written for temperate European climates — and it means something different in Accra.

Why Storage Conditions Matter

Heat, humidity and light can degrade active pharmaceutical ingredients — the compounds that actually make the medicine work. A degraded medication may not just lose potency; in some cases, degradation products can become harmful. Insulin stored at room temperature in Ghana for more than a few hours loses measurable effectiveness. Amoxicillin suspension reconstituted with water can spoil within days if not refrigerated.

25°C

is the maximum storage temperature for most medicines labelled "store in a cool, dry place" — often exceeded in Ghanaian homes.

3 days

is the maximum time reconstituted amoxicillin suspension remains effective at room temperature in Ghana's climate.

Room Temperature Medications

Most solid tablets and capsules can be stored at room temperature — but in Ghana this means actively choosing a cool spot, not simply leaving them anywhere. Avoid:

  • Bathroom cabinets (humidity from showers and baths is damaging)
  • Near the kitchen stove or refrigerator motor (heat sources)
  • Window sills or anywhere with direct sunlight
  • The dashboard or glove compartment of a car

Better options: a high shelf in an air-conditioned room, an insulated box away from heat sources, or a dedicated medicine drawer in a cool part of the house.

Best practice

Keep medicines in their original packaging. The blister packaging and amber bottles are specifically designed to block moisture and light. Transferring tablets to decorative containers or Ziploc bags removes this protection.

Medications That Must Be Refrigerated

  • Insulin — opened vials/pens should be kept in the fridge (2–8°C). Unopened insulin not yet in use should be refrigerated until needed.
  • Reconstituted antibiotic suspensions (amoxicillin, azithromycin dry syrup) — once mixed with water, refrigerate and use within the timeframe on the label.
  • Some eye drops — check the label; many require refrigeration after opening.
  • Suppositories — prone to melting in heat; always refrigerate.
  • Some vaccines and biologics — if prescribed for home use.

What to Do When Power Goes Out

1

Keep the fridge closed

A closed, well-stocked refrigerator will maintain safe temperatures for 4–6 hours without power.

2

Use a cool box with ice packs

For extended outages, transfer refrigerated medications to a cool box. This is especially important for insulin.

3

Do not refreeze thawed insulin

If insulin has warmed significantly and you are not sure whether it is still effective, contact a pharmacist before using it.

When in doubt, ask a pharmacist

If you are unsure whether a medicine has been compromised by heat or power outages, do not guess. HealthPlus pharmacists are available via the pharmacy page to answer questions about specific medications.

Safe Disposal of Expired Medicines

Do not flush medications down the toilet or throw them in the household bin, as they can contaminate water sources. Return expired medicines to your pharmacist for proper disposal, or crush solid tablets and mix them with an undesirable substance (used coffee grounds, dirt) before sealing in a bag for the bin.
